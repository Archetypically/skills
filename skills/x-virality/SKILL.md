# Engineer Virality on X

Plan an X launch or announcement post so other people have reasons to quote it, share it, and keep the conversation going.

## Shape the post so people can quote it

- Build the post around something specific a stranger can add to, such as the messy starting point, the result it produced, and what still had to be fixed by hand.

- Test the draft before posting by asking what a person who does not know the author could add. If nothing comes to mind, add a more specific detail, example, or result.

- Skip bare announcements. A post that only says a product exists gives readers nothing to discuss, and the conversation ends with congratulations from friends.

## Build several angles from one post

- Write out three to five angles the same post can support, such as the founder's story, the product's implications, a video reaction, a controversial take you can defend, and the competing interests the product creates.

- Give each person who shares the post one angle to own, so their quote adds something the other quotes do not.

- Weigh what a heated angle leaves people believing about the product. Aim for disagreement the team can answer with evidence.

## Match angles to people

- Pick sharers whose own experience fits the angle, such as a specialist who deals with the problem daily or a customer who can show results. Favor a provable fit with their audience over raw follower count.

- Read their recent posts and replies before reaching out, and check who pays attention to them and what questions come up.

- Brief each sharer in five short lines: what the product does, what they can test themselves, the part their audience would care about, the evidence behind the claim, and a request for their honest take after trying it.

- Offer a call if they need help understanding the product. Disclose paid partnerships, and let each sharer develop the angle in their own words.

## Work the response

- Split the conversations across the team before launch, covering replies on your own post, quote posts, and comments under sharers' posts. Agree on who answers product questions and who follows up on business interest.

- Answer where people commented. Move to a private message only when there is a clear reason, and check the person's profile and context first.

- Keep a shared document of recurring questions, objections, product issues, and unexpected use cases, with links to the conversations and the answers given. Feed the repeats into the FAQ, walkthroughs, and product fixes, and return to the thread when a fix ships.

- Share the strongest outside contributions, such as tests, results, and angles you missed. Ask permission first and credit the author.

## Plan the next wave from the results

- Judge the wave by product trials, relevant messages, and sales calls, not by views. Ask prospects where they found you when the source is unclear.

- Book a follow-up within a week or two with sharers who brought useful conversations, and give them something new to explore, such as a deeper walkthrough or a customer use case.

- Give recurring use cases more space in the next demo and on the landing page.

- Check the next launch against five items before it goes out: a clear claim with evidence behind it, angles matched to the people sharing them, sharers who understand the product and its audience, a team ready to answer questions and follow up, and an easy way to try the product or make contact.
